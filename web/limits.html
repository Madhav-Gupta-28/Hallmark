What this is not

Every limit below is in the specification, and they are collected here rather than buried at the bottom of a document nobody opens. One of them is not fixable from inside the design, and it is stated rather than managed.

Not claimed

Not best execution.

Hallmark proves a bound and enforces a realised cost, not optimality. With one filler and first-come-first-served, the filler delivers near the floor and keeps the spread: the owner gets their ceiling, not the best price in the market. An auction window — collect deliveries for N blocks, settle the best — is the natural next version and is not a v1 claim.

Not an independent price benchmark.

Killed by measurement, and it was the more compelling story. All 8,992 Rhea pools were scanned: 31 contain Intents-USDC, and zero contain Intents-USDC and wNEAR together. The cause is asset fragmentation — Intents uses omni-bridge tokens while Rhea's liquidity sits in factory.bridge.near wrappers and native tokens. Same tickers, different assets.

This is why the signed USD marks are the right reference: there is no on-chain alternative. The “benchmark 1Click against real liquidity” story was compelling and it was false.

Not a reputation or counterparty-scoring system.

No account-age heuristics, no blocklists, no contract-change detection. Six public NEAR repositories already do that, and all six pass the 99.99% quote.

Not a custodian, ever.

The output is in the vault before the input is credited to anybody, and payouts are pull rather than push. No design that involved holding a counterparty's funds survived review — there are eleven of them in the design ledger.

The honest hole

If 1Click's signing key is stolen, the cryptography is gone.

An attacker could mint quotes with any terms, and Hallmark would accept any that fit the ceiling. The guarantee degrades from cryptographic to the ceiling the owner signed — which still caps the loss at max_cost_bps of max_fill per fill rather than the whole escrow, but the crypto is gone.

This is not fixable from inside the design. It is stated rather than buried.

The weakest assumption the project is willing to make is the neighbouring one: that 1Click's signed amountInUsd and amountOutUsd marks are honest. The whole bound is denominated in them. If the venue marks dishonestly, the bound is wrong — but it is their own signature, which is why this is the weakest link we accept and why it is worth accepting.

Known limits, numbered

  1. One sub-cent transfer is not attributed. A fill charges the filler for the owner's credit slot, and the owner is credited ~0.00145 NEAR for releasing it. Never a loss to the vault, and it cannot be farmed, because every such transfer requires a real delivery that costs the filler far more than that.
  2. First-come-first-served means the filler delivers near the floor. See above.
  3. 1Click signer-key compromise is unmitigated. See above.
  4. No independent price benchmark exists. A bound, never best execution.
  5. Liveness depends on 1Click. If the API stops signing, Hallmark stops filling. It never stops refusing, which is the safe direction.
    Observed, not theorised. On 2026-11-02 the API returned HTTP 521 for an extended period, then partially recovered — serving /v0/tokens while refusing every quote with “Quoting for this pair is not available”. During the outage no fill was possible and every keyless view kept answering. It also found a gap on our side: posting a mandate, which needs nothing from the venue, failed with the outage because resolving a ticker went through /v0/tokens. Fixed — and the live mandate was posted during the outage to prove it.
  6. The filler needs capital. It must front the output. This is the deliberate cost of closing the depositAddress hole, and it is what a dealer does.
  7. Three fillers, all written by us or adapted by us. There is no third-party filler ecosystem and none is claimed.
  8. Immutable means unpatchable. Once the full-access key is deleted, a bug found later cannot be patched — only superseded by a new deployment that owners migrate to voluntarily.
  9. Mainnet only. No Intents testnet exists or is planned. Dev accounts, tiny amounts, dry:true quotes wherever possible.

Immutability is a deployment step, not a sentence

“Nobody to switch it off” is only true once it is executed. After the final deploy the full-access key gets deleted, so the contract cannot be upgraded or paused by anyone, including us. Two costs, both accepted and both stated:

  1. A bug found later cannot be patched, only superseded.
  2. The storage lock becomes permanent. An account with no full-access key cannot be deleted, and deleting the account is the only way to release the NEAR staked against the contract's code — 2.76 NEAR for this contract. Immutability and recoverability are the same key; you cannot keep both.

The key still exists, deliberately. This project found a critical bug in a contract that was already live, twice — and fixed both by redeploying. Had the key been gone, neither could have been fixed. Because the cost is identical whenever it is paid, the key is deleted last, after the hackathon's winners are announced.

↳ this is the clearest argument in the project for doing the irreversible thing late, and it is only available because the audit happened before the deadline rather than after it.

What success looks like, honestly

Not a billion-dollar company — a product with real usage.

The near-term test is whether anyone delegates a real order to a mandate they did not write, and whether a filler they do not know fills it inside the bound. That is a small number of people doing a real thing, and it is the only number worth watching first.

The larger shape, stated as a thesis and not a projection: if delegated machine execution becomes ordinary, something has to bound it, and the thing that bounds it is positioned to become the standard rather than an implementation. FINRA asking about safe harbors is the tell. Revenue, at 1Click's own 20 bps anchor against $4.80B a month of settled Intents volume, is a real business long before it is a large one. We are not claiming the large one.
