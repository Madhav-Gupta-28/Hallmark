The open book

Every mandate the vault currently holds, straight from get_mandates. A mandate is an order with its own bound welded on: the owner has escrowed the input and named the worst all-in cost they will accept. Any filler may take it, and the contract will refuse them if the arithmetic does not hold.

Price a fill

This is the whole mechanism in one action. Pick a live mandate, and the page fetches a real signed quote from NEAR Intents for that exact pair and size, then asks the vault what it would do with it. The contract verifies the venue's signature, reads four signed fields, and answers. No oracle is consulted and no price is taken on trust.

↳ the second button needs nothing from the venue — it replays a captured quote carrying a 500 bps hidden fee, so you can see a refusal even if 1Click is down.

Nothing has been asked of the chain yet. Press Get a live quote and this fills with the venue's signature, the four signed numbers, the two cost figures and the contract's own verdict — each one a keyless view call you can repeat yourself.

Post a mandate

Escrow an input token together with the worst cost you will accept. From that moment the bound is not a promise anyone made you — it is a condition the contract checks on every fill, and cannot be talked out of.

Open a mandate

Two transactions: prepay the storage your record will occupy, then transfer the input token to the vault with your terms in msg. The transfer is the escrow — there is no separate deposit step and no moment where the vault holds your funds without terms attached.

Why two signatures, and why a wallet

A NEAR function-call access key may only sign calls to the one contract it was granted for, and only with a zero attached deposit. Prepaying storage needs a deposit; the escrow transfer targets the token contract, not the vault, and needs one yoctoNEAR. Both rules are broken, in opposite directions — so no key this page could hold would let it act for you. That is a property of NEAR's permission model, and it is the right one: a page cannot be given a key that quietly moves your funds. Hence a wallet, or the command, your choice.

Your position

Everything the vault owes an account, and everything it is holding for one. Payouts are pull, never push: a fill credits you, and you withdraw when you like. A counterparty who is not registered on a token therefore cannot wedge a fill for anybody else.
